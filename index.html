Installation

Estación y camino

2026 · Installation — plastic stools, newspaper, variable spatial arrangement · Variable dimensions

Estación y camino offers a reflection on the invisible dynamics of informal labor and the repetitive journeys that sustain much of urban life.

The installation uses colorful plastic stools —common objects within the Latin American street landscape— as symbols of the street vendor and of the temporary structures that occupy public space every day. Arranged in a row, the stools build a trajectory that evokes the constant movement of the workplace, where each station represents a momentary pause within a routine marked by mobility, waiting and adaptation.

Beneath this path, a road made of newspaper functions as a symbolic surface over which these everyday economies travel. The printed news —a record of political, social and economic conflicts— lies literally beneath the passage of the stools, suggesting how informal labor continues to exist and resist amid the ceaseless flow of events that runs through society.

The piece seeks to make visible labor practices that are frequently normalized or ignored, understanding public space as a place of exchange, survival and collective memory.

Part of Intercambios cotidianos, the artist's first solo exhibition — Centro Cultural Sampedrano, San Pedro Sula, 2026.

About

Ninfa Espinoza (b. 1992) is a Honduran visual artist based in San Pedro Sula. In 2026 she completed her studies in Interior Design at CEUTEC. In 2023 she received an Honorable Mention as Revelation Artist at the XXXIII National Art Salon of the Centro Cultural Sampedrano.

In 2026 she presented Intercambios Cotidianos, her first solo exhibition, at the Centro Cultural Sampedrano. Her exhibition history includes participation in group shows at the national and international level, among them TE ODIO — presented in 2025 at the Centro Cultural de España in Costa Rica and in 2026 at the Centro Cultural de España in Tegucigalpa — as well as El Arte y la Paz, presented in 2025 at Torre Banpaís. She has taken part for three consecutive years in the National Art Salon of the Centro Cultural Sampedrano.

Since 2023 she has been a member of the Colectivo de Pintores Sampedranos and, since 2024, of the Asociación de Mujeres Artistas Hondureñas (AMAH), taking part in exhibitions, workshops and artistic training activities.

Her practice explores the relationship between people, everyday life and the spaces they inhabit. Through painting and installation, and using materials such as canvas, paper, plastic, wood and cotton, she addresses experiences linked to labor, affective bonds and urban environments, paying attention to vulnerability, resilience and the ways in which the everyday builds memory and identity.
